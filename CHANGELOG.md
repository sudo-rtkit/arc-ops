## 0.24.0 — Weapons and augment from screenshots, confirming values

- **Weapons and the augment are recognised on loadout and FINDINGS screenshots** (MANAGE LOADOUT, the post-raid FINDINGS screen and its automatic capture, and the loadout builder's screenshot import). Until now these slots were always “unknown”. New reader `src/core/vision/equip.ts`:
  - **Scale-normalised shape.** The weapon slot shows a large horizontal image, the augment a small icon, so the 16 × 16 item fingerprint (built for icons in a square cell at a fixed scale) does not fit. The foreground (bright pixels outside the rarity colour) is cropped to its bounding box, stretched to a 32 × 12 grid and kept with the box's aspect ratio; the same function computes the shape from the screenshot and from the catalogue icon, so resolution and UI scale do not matter. The augment shape gets a margin around the box, so a solid box is compared by its outline rather than its texture.
  - **Catalogue shapes.** New dev-only script `scripts/gen-equip.mjs` reads the cached MetaForge icons (`scripts/.cache/`, no network) and writes `src/core/data/equipShapes.ts` (26 weapons, 16 augments; names, rarity, base magazine size and shapes as numbers, no images, 53 kB). The catalogue's weapon icons are drawn at about 32°; the script straightens them (new `rotate` in `vision/image.ts`) and stores shapes for 30°, 32° and 34°.
  - **Decision, never a guess.** Candidates must have the slot's rarity, and for weapons a base magazine no larger than the one read from the ammo line (“0/20”; an attachment can only enlarge it). *High* = correlation ≥ 0.80, ≥ 0.07 ahead of the next item and an aspect ratio within 15 %; only then is the name written. *Medium* (≥ 0.75, ≥ 0.04 ahead) is only a **suggestion** for review that the player has to tick; below that the slot stays unknown. Every field carries its confidence (0–1).
  - **Tier** from the grey Roman numeral in the slot's bottom-right corner, using the existing tier shape logic (now shared as `tierOfShape` in `vision/digits.ts`). A weapon with a sure name but an unread tier is written without the tier and its slot goes to review.
  - **Per-field accuracy on the player's local screenshots** (9 screenshots; never committed, the test skips without them): augment 6/6 correct and sure (Free Loadout Augment, 0.956), tier 6/6 (I, I, II, I, IV, II), weapon name 2/6 sure (Kettle I 0.88, Ferro I 0.82), 1/6 correct suggestion below the threshold (Stitcher I 0.79, in-raid inventory), 3/6 unknown (Ferro II with another stock, Anvil IV with a silencer, Torrente II; the catalogue only has tier I icons and the attachments change the outline); the best candidate was the right weapon in 6/6, and **no field was ever named wrongly**. Ammo read 6/6 (0/20, 0/1, 0/1, 20/20, 0/6, 0/82).
  - **Where it goes.** The loadout builder's “From a screenshot” review shows the confidence per row, “tier not read” and a “Suggestion: … use it” button (never prefilled). The raid lifecycle (0.20.0) stores the recognised weapon with tier and augment in the loadout snapshot; the automatic FINDINGS capture now also reads the EQUIPMENT column and fills the equipment slots the pre-raid loadout did not know (or, without a seen loadout, the equipment alone; the backpack then stays unknown, so the loot still goes to review). Unrecognised gear goes to “To review” as rows with the suggestion and its confidence, unticked; a ticked row is added to the raid's gear (and its losses when knocked out) through a new review edit `gear`. The post-raid evaluation shows the gear recognised from screenshots with its catalogue value (kept or lost; items without a price listed separately).
- **Provenance follow-ups (0.21.0 “Not yet”).**
  - **Confirm per value:** an unconfirmed value (from automatic capture, an older version or a backup) has a small **Confirm** button next to its origin dot (stock, equipment, profile, station level, completed quest; not inside skill-tree nodes or objective labels, where a nested button would be invalid). Value, source, time and confidence stay; only “confirmed” changes. A raid entry with an open review item is not confirmed (the review does that).
  - **Settings → Data origin:** **“Confirm all in area”** per area with unconfirmed values.
  - Origin dots (the existing `ProvDot`) now also on the material counts in **workshop recipes** and in the **shopping list**.
  - Two new store actions `provConfirm` and `provConfirmArea`, both in the overlay relay allow-list (area from the fixed list, key at most 130 characters, both validated by the action). No new command, event, bus route or capability; no schema change (schema stays 19).
- **Small fixes noticed on the way:** (1) the scan review thumbnail of a weapon slot was cropped to a centre square and cut the weapon off; it now shows the whole slot; (2) item names in the “For loadouts” shopping table open the item card like the other shopping table; (3) `rotate`'s canvas no longer grows by a pixel from floating-point error at 90°.
- “What's new” entry for 0.24.0 (CS/EN).
- Tests: synthetic shape, rotation, rarity / magazine filtering, aspect rejection (silencer), Roman numerals I / II / IV and a full synthetic FINDINGS screen with drawn weapons (committed); the shape catalogue; equipment fields, FINDINGS gear merging, the gear review edit and normalisation of the stored lifecycle; provenance confirm per value and per area, including the relay; the post-raid gear value; the local per-field accuracy report. E2E in Czech and English: confirming a value from the shopping list, the dots on workshop and shopping materials, and “Confirm all in area”.
- Not yet: weapons whose look differs from the catalogue's tier I icon (higher tiers, silencers, stocks) stay unknown; recognising them would need per-tier icons (the cache only has tier I) or learning from the player's own confirmed screenshots. The hotkey screenshot of FINDINGS still reads only the backpack. Confirm buttons inside the skill tree and on quest objectives.

## 0.23.0 — Loadout builder and app load

- **Loadout builder (Weapons → Loadouts, store schema 19).** A loadout is built by its in-game slots: augment, shield, two weapons with an optional tier, BACKPACK, QUICK USE, AUGMENTED SLOTS and SAFE POCKET. Slot counts and the weight limit default to the game's MANAGE LOADOUT screens (custom loadout 20 / 5 / 1 / 3 slots and 30.0 kg, free loadout 14 / 4 slots, 35.0 kg, no safe pocket and no augmented slots) and stay editable, because the item catalogue does not say how an augment changes them. “Game defaults” restores them; switching the type to a free loadout drops items from slots it does not have. Augment and shield fields suggest the catalogue's augments and shields; item names stay in the game's English.
  - **Live summary:** total value from catalogue sell values (items without a price are listed and not added; a weapon without a tier has a price only when all its tiers share it, so nothing is guessed), total weight against the limit (over-limit flagged), slots used per section with stack sizes (full sections flagged), **availability** per item from Inventory stock and Equipment (a tierless name matches any tier, a tiered name only that tier; the same item in several slots is summed; missing rows highlighted) and **loss risk**: the value you lose if knocked out, everything except the safe pocket (shown with the protected value). A free loadout risks nothing from your stash.
  - **Shopping list:** “Add to shopping list” per loadout puts its missing items into Inventory → Shopping list (“For loadouts”, with the loadouts that need them; the same item across loadouts counts once at the largest missing number).
  - **Templates:** save and duplicate as before; with two or more loadouts a **Compare** panel shows value, risk, weight, missing units and used slots side by side with the difference, plus the contents of every slot.
  - **Import from screenshot:** “From a screenshot” in the builder reads a MANAGE LOADOUT or FINDINGS screenshot with the existing loadout reader and shows the same review table as loot from a screenshot (thumbnail, editable name, count, include). Only reviewed, named rows are taken, together with the read slot counts and weight limit; unrecognised slots stay empty until you name them. Nothing is saved until you save the loadout; the screenshot stays in memory.
  - **Raid prep and evaluation:** step 4 shows the chosen loadout's value, risk, weight and what is missing from your stash, with a link to the builder. The post-raid evaluation shows the loadout's value at risk and, with a matching journal entry, whether it was lost (knocked out) or brought back; the plan record saved to the journal carries `risk` (null when a price is unknown). The raid entry is still prefilled with the whole loadout, and loot from a screenshot now subtracts the loadout's quick-use items too.
  - **Migration:** older loadouts get the custom type (free when their purpose is “Free Loadout”), no tiers, empty new slots and the default counts. A backup with schema 20 or newer is refused as before.
- **Reader fix (0.22.1 “Not yet”).** The custom loadout's teal slot labels (“0/20”, “0/5”) are now read, and the empty “+” slots of a custom loadout are recognised as empty instead of one unknown item, so an empty custom loadout no longer goes to review. The LOADOUT line also yields the weight and its limit (“0.0/30.0”: read only in that exact form, otherwise unknown). Verified on the player's local screenshots (custom empty 20/5 and 30.0 kg, free 14/4 and 35.0 kg, FINDINGS 21.2/35.0); those screenshots are never committed and their tests skip without them.
- **App load (Settings → App load).** While the panel is shown (about every 2 s, only with the window visible) it shows the app process's CPU %, working set, private memory and peak working set, the automatic-capture tick timing (`ops_autostats`) and the number of widget windows with their refresh interval. New main-window-only command `app_load`: `GetCurrentProcess` (pseudo-handle) with `GetProcessTimes` and `GetProcessMemoryInfo`, read-only; no other process is opened or read and the game is never touched (the WebView2 processes of the app are not measured either; the panel says so and shows the window's JavaScript heap). **Game FPS impact cannot be measured** without touching the game; the panel describes a manual method (in-game FPS counter or GPU overlay, same spot, app running vs. quit).
  - **Measured on the development PC:** one App load query (`app_load`) costs about 1.2 µs, i.e. about 0.00006 % of one core at one query per 2 s; an automatic-capture tick (desktop header strip of a 2560-px-wide monitor, 1.8 MB, plus classification) averaged 33 ms (max 40 ms), about 1.7 % of one core at one tick per 2 s, only while capture is on and the game is in front; classifying a 4K strip alone takes about 1.5 ms; a loadout summary with 25 lines takes about 25 µs and the advisor about 0.13 ms on a fresh state. The running app's own CPU % and working set were not sampled in this build session (launching the desktop window would take focus from whatever is in front); read them in the panel.
- **Low-impact mode** (off by default, per PC): while the game is visible in the foreground, widgets get state at most every 2 s (instead of ~360 ms) and check the monitor every 10 s, Ops animations stop on their current frame, the silent automatic-capture emotes are skipped and the “What next?” advisor recomputes at most once a minute. Outside the game everything runs as before.
- **Field console hotkey** (Settings → Buddy and hotkey, optional, none by default): registered like the other hotkeys (RegisterHotKey, a new `console` slot of `ops_hotkey`, never the same as another shortcut). Pressing it opens the field console (App panel) with focus, on your explicit press only; pressing it again or closing returns focus to the game.
- **Widgets.** A **high-contrast** option (opaque graphite surfaces, no translucency, same layout; Settings → Widgets over the game). A separate **Hide / show** button next to a labelled **Edit** button under Ops (the design's split Ops entry): it only sends `widgets-hide` to the widget window through the new buddy-only command `widgets_hide`, never activates a window and leaves focus in the game. The tray menu has **Hide/show widgets** while widgets are on (handled in Rust, same event, the main window is not shown).
- **Focus guarantees from 0.22.1 unchanged:** the buddy still lets clicks through while the game is in front, Edit and the console take focus only on an explicit action, and the static guards cover the new paths (Hide / show, the tray item, the console hotkey branch).
- **Security.** Two new commands in `build.rs`: `app_load` (main only, `capabilities/main.json`) and `widgets_hide` (buddy only, `capabilities/buddy.json`). `windows-sys` gains `Win32_System_Threading` and `Win32_System_ProcessStatus`; `tests/safety-m5.test.ts` now pins that only `GetCurrentProcess`, `GetProcessTimes` and `GetProcessMemoryInfo` are used, once each, on the own pseudo-handle, and that no process, thread, priority or module API exists anywhere. No new Rust event, bus route or relay action; low-impact mode, the console hotkey and widget contrast are per-PC settings (not changed by a backup import, not reachable through the overlay relay).
- “What's new” entry for 0.23.0 (CS/EN); `versionForSchema(18)` = 0.22.1.
- Tests: unit tests for item facts, value, weight, slots, loss risk and availability, slot rules and type switching, comparison, the loadout shopping list, screenshot prefill (synthetic rows and the local screenshots), the schema 19 migration and backups, raid prep / evaluation risk, the custom-loadout reader fix and weight parsing, low-impact functions and hub behaviour, the console hotkey, per-PC settings, the own-process metric guard and Rust tests for the own-process query. E2E in Czech and English: building a loadout with availability and loss risk, the shopping list, duplicate and compare, raid prep, screenshot prefill and the App load panel.
- Not yet: slot counts per augment (the catalogue has no such data; counts stay editable); the weapon slot of a loadout screenshot stays unrecognised as before (named by hand in the review); the WebView2 processes are not included in the CPU/RAM numbers; low-impact mode uses the existing “game visible in front” signal, which also counts the app's own window over the game.

## 0.22.1 — The buddy no longer takes you out of the game

- **Bug fix (user report: “while I play, the buddy sometimes catches and I'm taken out of the game”).** Root cause: the 96 × 96 Ops window (created since 0.13.1 with `focused(false)` but *without* `focusable(false)`) was an ordinary, activatable, always-on-top window in the screen corner. In a borderless game the cursor can reach that corner (inventory, map, menus); a stray click activated the Ops window (Windows activates a normal window on click), the game lost focus and the click itself was lost, and the same click also toggled the field console, which takes focus by design. A press-and-move started a window drag of Ops (“it catches”). The speech bubble temporarily enlarges the window, so the clickable area grew while Ops talked. **Second root cause, found in the release smoke test:** the “show without activation” used since 0.13.1 for Ops and since 0.22.0 for the widget window (`SW_SHOWNOACTIVATE`, then Tauri `show()` so Tauri knows the window is visible) did activate. For a window it still considers hidden, tao 0.37 removes `WS_VISIBLE` and calls `ShowWindow(SW_SHOW)`, because the “don't focus” marker is cleared right after creation. Whenever the app was allowed to take the foreground (its own window in front, its hotkey just pressed, or no foreground window during a game transition), the new Ops or widget window became the foreground window, and with automatic display Ops is recreated every time the game comes back to the front. In the smoke test the Ops window became the foreground window right after it appeared. The other paths that can run during play (emotes, advisor speech, sleep and wake, What's new, tray updates, widget refit, the Hide / show shortcut, automatic capture) emit events or use `SWP_NOACTIVATE` only.
- **Fix.** Ops and the widget window are created visible with `focused(false)`, which tao shows during creation with `SW_SHOWNOACTIVATE`; `show()` is never called on them afterwards. Ops stays click-through and draws nothing until it has been placed, and `buddy_reveal` only marks it placed. The Ops window can no longer be activated (`focusable(false)`, `WS_EX_NOACTIVATE`). New setting **Settings → Buddy and hotkey → “Buddy lets clicks through to the game (recommended)”**, on by default: while the game is the foreground window, the Ops window ignores the cursor (`set_ignore_cursor_events(true)`), so every click goes to the game. The existing watch loop switches it by the foreground window (game → pass-through; this app, another program or the desktop → clickable as before; a brief “no window” keeps the current state). A new Ops window is set up immediately on creation. As a second line of defence, Rust refuses a click, drag or Edit request from Ops while the game is in the foreground (`buddy:pass`), so a click that arrived just before the switch still cannot take focus. During play you use the hotkeys (capture, widget Edit). The watch loop now runs whenever this setting is on (only the foreground window and process names are read, as before), including with automatic buddy display and automatic capture off.
- **Widgets.** Already click-through and non-activating in Play since 0.22.0; Edit now ends by itself when the game becomes the foreground window again (after a 1 s grace period), so the full-monitor widget window can never keep catching clicks over the game.
- **Security.** One new main-window-only command `ops_passthrough` (build.rs manifest, `capabilities/main.json`, ACL test). Only window APIs: no mouse or keyboard hooks, no input injection, no cursor position or key-state reads (pinned by a static test). “Hold Alt to make Ops clickable” is **not** implemented: it would need a key-state read (`GetAsyncKeyState`), a new input API. The setting belongs to this PC (not in backups, a backup import never changes it, the field console cannot change it via the relay).
- **Recognition.** Both MANAGE LOADOUT tabs (CUSTOM LOADOUT with an empty loadout, FREE LOADOUT) already matched the loadout header (score 1.00, also at 720p). The in-raid inventory (INVENTORY · CRAFTING · MAP · LOGBOOK · SYSTEM) is now recognised by three separate probes outside the capture screen list: it never triggers a capture and never counts as a screen; it only moves the raid lifecycle from “loadout seen” straight to “in raid” (`auto-screen` value `inRaid`, life event `inRaid`). In-raid gameplay with the HUD matches nothing. New local-only regression samples (a second FINDINGS and KNOCKED OUT screen, both MANAGE LOADOUT tabs, the in-raid inventory and HUD) are never committed; tests skip without them.
- Tests: Rust unit tests for the pass-through decision (game / own window / other / none), the click refusal and the Edit end; Rust header tests on 31 own samples (all correct, also at 720p) and synthetic in-raid strips at five resolutions that never fire `AutoGate`; static guards that forbid activating calls in every path that runs during play; hub tests for the setting, the watch loop and the per-PC rule; an E2E test for the setting in both languages; reader regression tests for the new samples.
- Not yet: Alt-to-click (see above); the custom-loadout reader does not measure the 0/20 backpack and 0/5 quick use of a custom loadout and reads its empty “+” slots as one unknown item (the result then goes to review, nothing is guessed).

## 0.22.0 — Widgets over the game

Design: [docs/design/overlay-widgets](docs/design/overlay-widgets/) (start with `review.html`). The earlier full-screen console direction in `docs/design/overlay-v2` was rejected and is not built.

- **Security fix (Codex Security scan, CWE-407, medium):** a crafted backup with hundreds of thousands of raids and no provenance froze the main window during the migration step, before the import preview, because each raid's time was looked up with a linear search (quadratic work). The migration now uses a map (linear). Regression test with 300,000 raids.

- **Widgets over the game (optional, off by default, pending Embark review).** Settings → Widgets over the game (needs the Buddy and hotkey switch). Ten widgets, each fed by the existing core logic: To find (active raid plan, `raidList`, loot-area chips), Pinned quests (pinned quests with objective ticks), Shopping (the compact window's `compactShopping` for the next upgrade), What next? (top advisor tip), Keep / sell / recycle (`lookupAdvice`; the search field exists only in Edit, Play shows the last explicit result), Stash capacity (last confirmed STASH slots, stale after 14 days), Weekly challenges (your recorded stars), Ops (level and form, no animation or unsolicited speech), To review (the automatic-capture review queue plus screenshots waiting for review) and Automatic capture (running / paused / off). No map, position or minimap widget. Item names stay in the game's English. Sizes, minimum and maximum per widget follow the design registry; compact and expanded densities; rows that do not fit end with “+ n more”.
- **Modes.** *Play*: widgets are display-only, the shared window ignores the cursor (`set_ignore_cursor_events(true)`) and cannot be activated (`focusable(false)`, `WS_EX_NOACTIVATE`), there is nothing focusable in the DOM and no dimming; data refreshes never request focus. *Edit*: opened only by clicking Ops, the optional Edit shortcut or Settings → Edit widgets; a 20 % veil, the toolbar (+ Widget, Layouts, Undo, Reset, App, Search · Ctrl+K, Hide all, Done · Esc), move bars, eight resize handles, the catalogue, per-widget settings and layouts. Esc first cancels a drag, then closes a popup, then leaves Edit; Done always leaves. On leaving, the window ignores the cursor again and focus goes back to the window that was in front before Edit (the game, also when Edit was opened by clicking Ops), only if focus still belongs to the companion (an Alt+Tab elsewhere is respected). “Hide all” hides every widget for the session; an optional Hide / show shortcut toggles it in Play, and entering Edit shows them again.
- **Per-widget settings.** Position by drag with snapping to the monitor edges, the 24 px safe inset, the monitor centre and other widgets' edges and centres within 8 px (nearest wins, ties prefer edges), otherwise an 8 px grid; Alt bypasses snapping; one vertical and one horizontal guide. Size by eight handles in 8 px steps within the widget's min / max. Keyboard: arrows move 8 px (Shift 1 px), Ctrl+arrows resize, Delete removes; numeric width, height and position fields. Opacity 30–100 % (default 85 %) applies to the frame; the surface directly behind text never drops below 90 %. Compact / expanded, lock, bring forward / send backward. Undo / Redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z) within the Edit session.
- **Layouts.** Shipped templates “Raid” (to find + quests, top-left) and “Lobby” (What next? + shopping left, quests + stash top-right); personal changes override a template and Reset restores it (undoable). Save as… (unique name, 1–40 characters), rename and delete custom layouts (up to 12); one click switches. Optional “Follow game state automatically” (off by default) switches Lobby / Raid on the 0.20.0 automatic-capture raid phase transitions, only while capture is active, deferred until Edit ends; a manual choice pauses it until re-enabled. Every widget stores its nearest anchor (left / centre / right, top / centre / bottom) and offset in logical px, so layouts keep their sizes and edge distances across 1080p, 1440p, ultrawide and DPI changes and are clamped into the monitor.
- **Per-PC, not in backups.** Layouts live only in the widget window's local webview storage (`arcops:widgets`, versioned, validated on read, one atomic write); they are not part of the app state, so backups never contain them and a backup import never changes them. The widget switch and the two widget shortcuts are per-PC `opsPrefs.widgets`, kept on import like the other Ops preferences; the overlay cannot change them through the relay (`setOpsPrefs` ignores them, the new `setWidgets` is main-window only). Store schema stays 18.
- **Window architecture.** One transparent, undecorated, always-on-top, skip-taskbar `widgets` window sized to the game's monitor (the largest visible game window's monitor, else Ops's monitor, else the primary), re-fitted without activation on resolution, DPI or monitor changes; excluded from screen captures like the other Ops windows. The existing 96 × 96 Ops (buddy) window is the input island that receives clicks while the widget window lets them through. The full field console window from 0.16.0 is unchanged and becomes the **App** panel: its tabs (Overview, Quests, Profile, Inventory, Workshop, Skills, Shopping, Scan, Raid) and every full module stay there, because none maps 1:1 to a display-only widget (they are interactive views; screenshot review still opens there). It opens from Edit → App (optionally straight on a module from Ctrl+K), and as before from the main window's capture button; with widgets off, clicking Ops still toggles it.
- **Security (0.16.1 model).** New `capabilities/widgets.json` with only event listening, `bus_send`, `widgets_edit`, `widgets_fit` and `widgets_app`. New commands in `build.rs`: `widgets_open` / `widgets_close` (main only), `widgets_edit` (main, Ops, widgets), `widgets_fit` and `widgets_app` (widgets only, validated tab / module). `ops_hotkey` takes an optional slot (capture, edit, hide) and refuses a shortcut already used by another slot. New bus routes only main ↔ widgets; the widget window may send only `widgetAction` with `questObjective` or `setRaidFound` and fixed argument limits (`WIDGET_ACTIONS` in `store/relay.ts`), applied by the same store actions. New Rust-only events `widgets-mode` and `widgets-hide`. No new process, memory or input API; nothing is sent to the game.
- Focus restore after the field console now also returns to the game when the console was opened by clicking Ops (the clicked Ops window is no longer remembered as the previous window).
- Housekeeping: the repository is public; the CI comment and the CI badge note are updated.
- Tests: unit tests for layout persistence, anchoring and resolution changes, snapping, presets, the opacity floor and the mode state machine, hub tests for the widget window, shortcuts and the action allow-list, Rust tests for the App tab filter and the new bus routes, ACL pins for the new window, commands, routes and events, and E2E in Czech and English (Play without controls, Edit, catalogue, move / resize, Save as, preset switch, persistence after reload and a resolution change, Hide all and the Hide / show shortcut, the App panel and Ctrl+K opening modules, the Settings switch).
- Not yet: a high-contrast widget option, a separate non-activating Hide / show button on the Ops window (the design's split Ops entry), a tray menu item for Hide / show, and screenshots of the widgets in the READMEs (the designer will refresh them).

## 0.21.0 — CI and the origin of every value

- **Continuous integration on GitHub Actions** (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`), on every push to `main` and on pull requests, with a read-only token, no secrets and a concurrency group that cancels superseded runs. An Ubuntu job installs with pnpm (cached), runs `tsc`, Vitest and `pnpm build`, then the Playwright E2E suite against the built app in Playwright's Chromium (`playwright.config.ts` keeps the locally installed Microsoft Edge and switches to Chromium when `CI` is set; `PW_CHANNEL=chromium` forces it locally). In CI a flaky E2E test gets one retry, and the HTML report plus traces are uploaded as an artifact on failure. A Windows job runs `cargo test --locked` in `src-tauri` with the cargo cache, so the Windows-only `ops.rs` code compiles there; it uses a placeholder `dist/` because `generate_context!` embeds the frontend at compile time. The NSIS installer is not built in CI. Tests that need the player's local screenshots (`tests/fixtures/vision/own/`, not in the repository) skip themselves. CI status badge in both READMEs (0.22.0: the repository is public, so the badge renders for everyone).
- **Provenance of every acquired value** (`src/core/provenance.ts`, store schema **18**). For stock counts, equipment, profile fields (level, XP, coins…), workshop station levels, skill ranks, completed quests, quest objectives, stash fullness and Raid Journal entries the app keeps the latest record: source (`manual`, `screenshot-hotkey`, `screenshot-file`, `auto-capture`, `backup-import`, `migration`; no record = `default`), time, recognition confidence (0–1, or none) and whether the player confirmed it, plus the previous source when it changed. A short log keeps the last 50 write batches (manual clicks within a minute merge).
  - **Wiring:** the store compares the tracked areas before and after every action and stamps what changed with the action's source, so every manual edit (including edits relayed from the overlay) is covered without per-action code. Imports pass their own source: the STASH table (file in the app, hotkey, or pages that came only from automatic capture), FINDINGS loot, progress screenshots and the automatic raid entry. STASH confidence is the recognition score of the row, capped at 50 % when the count was uncertain; values from the confirmation tables count as confirmed. An automatic raid entry stays unconfirmed until its “To review” items are resolved (including dismissing them) or it is edited with nothing left in the queue; confirmation keeps its source and time. A backup import stamps every value as `backup-import` and keeps the backup's own source as the previous one; resetting a module drops its records. The main window sets source and time; the overlay relay still carries only an allow-listed action name and data, so the overlay cannot supply either (pinned by a test). No new relay actions, permissions or commands.
  - **Migration:** a state without provenance (schema ≤ 17) gets `migration` records for every value that differs from the default, with an unknown time except where the state knows it (quest completion, the STASH header, raid time).
  - **Size:** at most 3,000 records per area (oldest dropped), compact keys; 500 materials read from a screenshot add well under 100 kB, far below the 20 MB save limit.
- **UI:** a small dot next to the value with the origin in its tooltip and accessible label (for example “From a screenshot (file) 13 Nov 2026 15:02 · confidence 97 % · confirmed”, “Manually 12 Nov 2026 14:20”) in Inventory (stock and equipment), Profile, Workshop station levels, Skills, quest objectives and completed quests. Dot colour only adds to the text (muted manual, accent screenshot, outline migration/backup, amber unconfirmed, dashed default). Default values show no dot, except the profile and station fields. ## 0.16.1 — Security hardening

### Bezpečnost (security)

Fixes from a read-only security review of 0.15.0, extended to the 0.16.0 overlay relay. None was exploitable remotely: there is no remote content, no network at runtime and a strict CSP. These are defence-in-depth and robustness fixes.

- **Image memory budget.** Dimensions are now read from the file header (PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, BMP) before any decode. Screen import, the map screenshot and map screenshots from a backup are limited to **8192 px per side and 40 MP** (about 160 MB of RGBA). A larger image, or one whose header cannot be read, is refused with a clear message (CS/EN) and nothing changes; a small file with huge dimensions can no longer exhaust memory. Map screenshots from a backup go through the same check and the same 4096 px downscale as a normal map import; their size comes from the real image, not from the backup. A stored screenshot over the budget is not opened. Screen import keeps at most 12 screenshots at once.
- **Least privilege per window.** `build.rs` now declares every app command (Tauri 2 app manifest), so a command works only for windows whose capability grants it. Each window has its own capability without `core:default`: the main window can only listen to events and call its own commands; the buddy and the overlay can listen, read monitors, send messages to the main window and call the few commands they use. Tray, menu, image-from-file, path and JS-side window move/resize/show permissions are gone everywhere. The buddy and the overlay set their own size and position through `win_place` (and the buddy drags through `win_drag`); Rust applies these only to the calling window. Sensitive commands also check the caller's window label (state, snapshots, data folder, launch info, focus, capture, overlay open/close/toggle, links).
- **Events cannot be spoofed.** No window may emit events from JavaScript. Messages between windows go through the `bus_send` command: Rust stamps the real sender, allows only main ↔ buddy and main ↔ overlay, and limits the type name and size. Events only Rust may send (tray menu, window close, game running/visible, capture, overlay open/closed) use a separate `native:` channel that no window can produce. The main window accepts each message only from the window that may send it (for example overlay edits only from the overlay).
- **Overlay relay.** Action names are checked against a fixed set and must be own functions of the store actions; argument strings, lists and object sizes are limited in addition to the existing 256 K total.
- **`__proto__`, `constructor` and `prototype` keys** are dropped when the saved state, a snapshot or a backup is read, and every normalizer skips them, so a crafted backup can no longer swap the prototype of individual records. Window-message checks use `Object.hasOwn` instead of `in`.
- **Save limit equals the load limit (20 MB).** A state that would be too large to load is refused at save time with a warning (the last saved file stays, the app no longer rolls back silently to the older `.bak` on the next start); the same applies to snapshots and to the raw import copy. Inventory entries are capped at 5000.
- **Raw imports** in `imports/` are pruned to the newest 10, matching the browser build.
- **Export** writes through a uniquely named temporary file in the target folder and renames it atomically, so an unrelated `<name>.tmp` there is never overwritten.
- **Capture:** asking for a capture with an old id no longer discards the newer pending capture.
- **Focus restore** after closing the overlay checks that the remembered window still belongs to the same process; otherwise focus is not restored (no input is ever sent).
- **Privacy:** removed absolute local paths from a committed design preview and a personal first name from the prototype demo.
- No schema change (14). New tests pin the per-window permission sets, the command manifest, the label checks and the native event channel, so any broadening fails the test suite.

## 0.15.0 — Post-raid evaluation and plan history

- **After the raid (Raid prep, step 5).** With an active plan, the app compares what you found with the plan: found versus needed per item, what is still missing, and newly completed requirements (e.g. “Gear Bench 2: all materials ready”, “Quest: delivery items complete”). It appears on its own once a FINDINGS capture has been confirmed into a raid entry, and in the overlay's capture result right after confirming. Without a capture, **Evaluate raid** uses the stock change since the plan started.
- Starting a plan now snapshots the stock of the listed items and which goals were still missing something. Found = the higher of the stock increase and the loot recorded for the raid (never the sum), so loot moved to Inventory is not counted twice. The evaluation only reads: inventory still changes only through the existing confirmed capture or raid-log flows.
- **Next raid:** the same goals recalculated over the stock after the raid, with the same raid prep list and map recommendation (with reasons); one click starts the next plan with a fresh snapshot.
- Ops sums the result up in your chosen personality (CS/EN, two variants each per personality): everything found, partial, nothing found, and upgrade ready. In the overlay it uses the existing status line after you confirm the loot; nothing pops up over the game.
- **Raid history in the Raid Journal (no separate log).** “Save to journal” attaches the plan to the raid entry from the capture, or to the raid logged since the plan started; otherwise it opens the usual raid log with the plan attached. Logging a raid while a plan is active offers “Save the active plan with this entry” (on by default). The entry stores the goals, chosen map, loadout, found-from-plan counts, completed requirements and an estimated value of the found plan items (catalogue sell value, when known). A plan is stored with one entry only. Journal entries with a plan show a “Plan found/needed” badge and a **Plan** detail; older entries remain valid.
- **Statistics: “Which maps help with your goals”** per map with data: planned raids, average plan units found per raid and how often the plan was fulfilled (everything found, or a requirement completed). Fewer than 3 planned raids on a map are labelled “little data”.
- Raid prep's map score uses this history as one more readable reason (“on average 3 plan units per raid (5 planned raids)”, +1 when the average is at least one unit), only from 3 planned raids on that map.
- Schema 14: `raidPlan.snap` / `raidPlan.open` and the optional `raids[].plan`. Older states and backups load without loss; a plan started in 0.14.0 has no snapshot, so a capture-based evaluation reconstructs the stock before the raid from the loot already moved to Inventory, and the manual evaluation asks you to restart the plan. Newer schema 15 is rejected. Material renames also cover plan items.
- Not included: an evaluation for STASH captures (the stock-based evaluation covers them), and assigning loot areas to maps.
